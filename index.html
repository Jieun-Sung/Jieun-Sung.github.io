About

I am an integrated M.S.–Ph.D. candidate in Bioinformatics at Ewha Womans University, advised by Prof. Wankyu Kim in the Systems Pharmacology Laboratory. My work is supported by an NRF Ph.D. Student Research Promotion Grant, for which I serve as principal investigator.

My research applies deep learning to single-cell perturbation biology: predicting how a transcriptome shifts under genetic or pharmacological perturbation, and tracing those shifts back to the targets that caused them. Building models that make in silico perturbation experiments genuinely useful to drug discovery is what I find most interesting about this field, and I intend to keep working at that intersection of machine learning and pharmacology.

Selected work

ICML 2026 2026

scDEBART

A BART encoder–decoder foundation model for single-cell perturbation response. It reframes the pretraining objective from reconstructing absolute expression to predicting log fold-changes conditioned on basal expression, which lets it generalize inductively to perturbations it has never seen. Pretrained on a corpus I curated from 617 datasets, on 4×H100 with DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 (452 GPU-hours), and evaluated across CRISPRi and CRISPRa datasets.

  • 6.28M DE profiles / 66.6M cells
  • 4–7× top-gene recovery
  • 71.4% top-1 reverse perturbation

Under review 2026

TDEP

Maps drug-induced transcriptomic signatures onto target-specific perturbation responses, so that gene-level target effects can be inferred in silico from large-scale pharmacological data. Benchmarked against training-free retrieval baselines at both profile and pair level.

  • AUROC 0.846 profile
  • AUROC 0.873 pair
  • 1-NN baseline 0.671 / 0.729

In preparation 2026

FateDriver

A dual-view graph neural network that models cell-state transitions and gene regulatory structure jointly to identify candidate driver genes of transcription factor-induced cellular reprogramming.
